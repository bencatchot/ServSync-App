# Job expenses: private capture and direct-cost review

**Status: isolated interaction prototype, not a deployed financial ledger.** The expense review entry contains synthetic identities and sample work, with data local to memory. Reloading clears it. It is built only for this feature branch's Vercel Previews (or served by local Vite). It makes no service calls, writes no browser storage, and adds no shipping app route. Never use real receipts or customer data in the preview. Role switching is a review control, **not authorization**.

## User decisions and scope

- Owner/Admin/Office should record private actual expenses against an estimate or its job. When a job originates from an estimate, display one ledger, not cloned expenses. Direct job expenses without an estimate still count, but have no invented revenue projection.
- A field technician should be able to enter an amount and submit a receipt, without access to profit, other employees' costs, receipt history, or manager correction. Manual capture does not require a photo. Browser camera support and HEIC conversion depend on device; demonstrate JPG/PNG photo input, not OCR.
- Customers have no cost/receipt path, sharing toggle or billable-line action. These are never attachments to an estimate, invoice, report, homeowner library, Home History, guest view or customer PDF. Do not extend generic attachments or add selectable cost columns to customer-visible records.
- Distinguish actual direct cost from Price Book estimated cost. The example subtracts actual recorded direct cost from accepted pre-tax estimate revenue, not from invoice plus deposit or cash collected. It is a projection, not company net income; unknown quote yields no projection. Before adding billed/collected profit views, reconcile progress/deposit/final credits, discounts, voids and taxes.
- The review lets management correct an erroneous cost by excluding it from totals and entering its replacement. The real ledger should retain an internal audit trail of corrections. No production financial records are changed.

## Supported vs unsupported assignment

The ordinary job is an inspection. Its existing write helper in `servsync-job-lifecycle.sql` checks contractor/team role, not an individual job assignment. The similarly named local job assignment trigger guards local customer/property linkage, not technician assignment. Project Collaboration authorities are for project leadership and cannot substitute for a field technician's per-job assignment. Do not infer assignment from being the creator or from being in the company.

**Proposed gate:** before building the real backend, confirm whether expense submission is restricted to assigned jobs. If so, use an authoritative existing assignment if verified, else add a minimal manager-controlled per-job tech assignment (choose the tech when dispatching, not a second step each time a receipt is filed). If the owner instead intends active technicians to submit from all jobs in their company, accept that broader scope only with explicit approval. Neither choice lets a tech list others' entries or read gross profit. The prototype assumes one assigned fictional tech and defaults to denial outside that fixture.

## Proposed durable boundary, not applied

1. Derive identity, current contractor and active role server-side per request. Require scope-specific membership for technicians, and check assignment again on submission and file finalization. Managers require exact tenant and existing financial-management authority. A homeowner/customer context must not read financial data, including through shared document payloads. Never trust a client-supplied role or contractor ID.
2. Create a private expense ledger separate from homeowner-readable operational tables. Anchor estimate-originated entries to the original approved work lineage and make both the linked estimate and job resolve the same ledger. Reject ambiguous links, mismatched contractor IDs and unrelated estimate/job pairs. Independent jobs use their own anchor. Do not silently merge revisions. Use integer cents, actor/date, category, vendor, archive/replacement references and an idempotency key. Enforce uniqueness for concurrent retry.
3. Use a purpose-specific **private** receipt bucket and deny unauthenticated access, reads/listing by techs or homeowners and cross-tenant operations. Authorize short-lived uploads for a server-selected object only; validate type/size from the received bytes, normalize/strip metadata if supported, and bind it to the exact expense. Reconfirm actor/assignment after upload. Keep the original out of client logs, analytics, generic file lists and customer links. Use authenticated manager-scoped download; never persist a public URL. Plan failed-upload cleanup and revocation/retention before real receipts.
4. Migrate/test only after explicit approval for those protected changes. Use synthetic identities and independent server/database tests: management in tenant, active assigned tech write-only, unassigned/revoked/inactive tech, viewer, homeowner, different tenant and unauthenticated calls; verify private document contents are absent from existing customer responses/PDFs. Capture, retry, conversion, readback, actor changes, partial upload and invalid images need end-to-end tests before real collection.
